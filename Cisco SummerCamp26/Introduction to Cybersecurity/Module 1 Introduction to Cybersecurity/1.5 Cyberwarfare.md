# 1.5.1 The Purpose of Cyberwarfare

Cyberwarfare is the use of cyberattacks by a nation or state-sponsored group to achieve **political, military, economic, or strategic objectives** against another nation or organization.

Unlike traditional warfare, cyberwarfare does not always involve soldiers, tanks, or missiles. Instead, attackers use computers, malware, espionage, and network attacks to weaken or disrupt their targets.

The ultimate goal is usually to **gain an advantage over an adversary without engaging in direct physical conflict.**

## Gathering Intelligence and Stealing Secrets

One of the primary goals of cyberwarfare is **cyber espionage**.

Nations may attempt to steal:

- Military plans
- Defense secrets
- Government intelligence
- Research and development data
- Advanced technologies
- Classified information

By stealing this information, a country can:

- Strengthen its military capabilities.
- Reduce the technological advantage of rival nations.
- Gain valuable intelligence about future plans or strategies.
- Save years of research and development.

Stolen information can also be used for **blackmail or political pressure** against government officials or organizations.

### Example

If a nation steals classified military documents from another country, it may gain critical intelligence without firing a single shot.

## Attacking Critical Infrastructure

Cyberwarfare can also target a country's **critical infrastructure**.

Critical infrastructure includes systems that society depends on every day, such as:

- Power grids
- Water treatment facilities
- Hospitals
- Transportation systems
- Communication networks
- Banking systems
- Internet services

Disrupting these services can create widespread chaos and severely impact normal life.

### Example: Power Grid Attack

If attackers successfully shut down a major city's power grid:

- Traffic lights could stop working.
- Hospitals might struggle to provide emergency care.
- Businesses could be forced to close.
- Internet services could fail.
- Supply chains and transportation could be disrupted.

A single cyberattack could affect millions of people without causing direct physical destruction.

## Destabilizing a Nation

Cyberwarfare is not always about stealing information or shutting down systems.

Sometimes the goal is to **create fear, confusion, and distrust**.

A successful cyber campaign may:

- Disrupt the economy.
- Interrupt public services.
- Damage public confidence.
- Spread misinformation.
- Create political instability.

If citizens lose trust in their government's ability to protect them, the attacker has achieved a strategic advantage.

## The Biggest Lesson

One of the most important ideas in cyberwarfare is:

> A nation can be seriously harmed without a single soldier crossing its borders.

Modern warfare is no longer limited to land, sea, or air.

Cyberspace has become another battlefield.

## Things to Remember

- Cyberwarfare is the use of cyberattacks to gain an advantage over another nation or adversary.
- Cyber espionage focuses on stealing sensitive information and defense secrets.
- Critical infrastructure attacks aim to disrupt essential services like electricity, healthcare, transportation, and communications.
- Cyberwarfare can destabilize economies and reduce public trust in governments.
- A nation can suffer significant damage even without a traditional military attack.
