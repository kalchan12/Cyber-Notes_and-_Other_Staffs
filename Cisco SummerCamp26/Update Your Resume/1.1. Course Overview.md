# 1.1 Course Overview

## Introduction to Resume Optimization
Your resume is a primary marketing tool for your professional experience and skills. Before a human hiring manager sees it, it must often pass through an Applicant Tracking System (ATS), which filters candidates based on specific keywords and criteria. This guide outlines how to strategically enhance and customize your resume using AI tools while ensuring your information remains private and accurate.

### Course Goals
*   **Enhance Bullet Points:** Extract and synthesize information from source documents using local Large Language Models (LLMs).
*   **Memory Recall:** Use LLM-assisted self-interviewing to recall past accomplishments when source documents are missing.
*   **Tailoring:** Customize your resume to match specific job postings and requirements.
*   **Systematic Evaluation:** Verify AI-generated content for authenticity and accuracy.

### 5 Guiding Principles
1.  **Privacy:** Protect sensitive data by using local LLMs and de-identifying information before using public AI tools.
2.  **Prototyping:** Experiment with different AI prompts, critique the results, and iterate to improve your process.
3.  **Structured Process:** Work methodically, tackling your resume section by section and reviewing line by line.
4.  **Focused Context:** Keep project accomplishments distinct to avoid cross-project hallucination.
5.  **Systematic Review:** Combine LLM self-checks with human evaluation to ensure absolute accuracy.



# 1.2 Plan Your Process

## 1.2.2 Tool Selection: Local LLMs vs. Public Chatbots

When deciding which AI tool to use, you must weigh privacy against performance.

### Local LLMs (e.g., LM Studio, GPT4All, Ollama)
*   **Privacy:** High. Data stays on your machine, making them safe for sensitive or confidential work documents.
*   **Usage Limits:** Unlimited usage with no daily limits or paywalls.
*   **Performance:** Constrained by your computer's RAM and CPU/GPU. They run compressed (quantized) versions of large models. 
    *   *Tip:* If you have 8GB of RAM, stick to models that are 4GB or smaller (approx. 4 billion parameters). MacBooks with M-series chips handle these efficiently due to built-in GPUs.

### Public Chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini)
*   **Privacy:** Low. Prompts and data might be used to train future models. Never input sensitive work data.
*   **Performance:** Highly capable, fast, and able to handle complex reasoning tasks.
*   **Usage Limits:** Free tiers often restrict the number of prompts per day.

### Best Practice
*   **Default to Local LLMs:** Start here to protect privacy.
*   **Hybrid Approach:** Use a local LLM to flag and remove sensitive information. Once the data is sanitized (e.g., replacing real names with placeholders), you can pass it to a public chatbot for complex formatting or synthesis.



## Model Characteristics

*   **Reasoning vs. Non-Reasoning Models:**
    *   *Reasoning Models:* Generate a "scratchpad" of thought before answering. They are slower but handle complex tasks better.
    *   *Non-Reasoning Models:* Output responses immediately. Better for simple, straightforward tasks.
*   **Context Window:** 
    *   This is the model's "short-term memory." Overloading the context window can crash your machine if it exceeds your RAM.
    *   *Effective Context:* Even if a model can handle huge inputs physically, its "effective" context is usually smaller. It might forget earlier details or hallucinate. Keep contexts focused and start new chats frequently.



## 1.2.4 Processing Approaches: Bulk vs. Section-by-Section

When enhancing your resume, avoid giving the LLM the entire resume at once (Bulk Processing). This risks "spillover," where accomplishments from one job are mistakenly attributed to another.

### Recommended: Section-by-Section (End-to-End)
*   Process a single section of your resume completely (extracting, synthesizing, and refining) before moving on.
*   This approach allows you to prototype your prompts, catch mistakes early, and provide the LLM with highly focused context.

## 1.2.5 Identifying Task Dependencies
Plan ahead. When enhancing a specific job experience, the LLM will generate many detailed bullet points and identify various skills.
*   **Save the leftovers:** Keep the longer, unedited bullet points and lists of skills in a separate "Resume Notes" document.
*   **Future use:** These notes become the foundation for updating your resume's dedicated "Skills" section later, and provide rich material for tailoring your resume to specific jobs or preparing for interviews.


# 1.3 Add new content using a source document

## 1.3.1 Extracting Information from Source Documents

When creating a new resume section, you often rely on source documents (like monthly progress reports or performance reviews). Instead of reading these manually, you can prompt an LLM to synthesize them.

### 1.3.5 Privacy First
1.  **Prompt the Local LLM:** Ask it to identify any sensitive or private information (e.g., internal restructuring, layoffs, unreleased projects, or colleague names).
2.  **Output Format:** Request a table with three columns: the direct quote, the reason it is sensitive, and a suggested rewrite.
3.  **Sanitize:** Use this table to omit or rewrite sensitive details before placing the information in your resume or pasting it into public chatbots.

### 1.3.4 Effective Information Extraction
1.  **Extract Direct Quotes:** Ask the LLM to pull relevant information as word-for-word quotes. This minimizes hallucinations compared to asking it to write final bullet points right away.
2.  **Capture Metadata:** Always ask the LLM to extract relationships. For every accomplishment, pull the associated:
    *   Project name
    *   Task description
    *   Start and end dates
    *   List of relevant skills and tools
3.  **Organize by Project:** Work is usually project-based. Ask the LLM to group the extracted quotes under their respective project names.

### 1.3.3 Managing AI Chats: Prototyping and Branching
*   **The Problem with Overwriting:** If you edit an old prompt and regenerate it, you lose the previous response, making comparisons impossible.
*   **The Solution:** Use your tool is **Branching** or **Duplication** feature. Branch at the prompt you want to edit. This creates a parallel chat, allowing you to test different prompts side-by-side. Remember to save the revised prompt before generating the new response.



# 1.4 Enrich existing bullet points

## 1.3.6 Context Optimization
More context is not always better. Irrelevant information can confuse the LLM.
*   **Filter Output:** Tell the LLM to remove projects you do not want on your resume (e.g., side projects, confidential initiatives).
*   **Clean Up Annotations:** Ask the LLM to remove its own self-check commentary and internal notes to produce clean text.
*   **Use a Diff Tool:** Use a tool (like Visual Studio Code is Compare feature) to compare document versions line-by-line. This catches unexpected deletions. Red means removed/changed; Green means added/modified.

## 1.3.7 Writing and Checking Resume Bullet Points
Once you have clean, project-specific quotes, you can ask the LLM to draft resume bullet points.
*   **Provide Guidelines:** Instruct the LLM to start sentences with an action verb, avoid "I", and include facts and numbers.
*   **1.3.8 LLM Self-Check:** LLMs hallucinate often when drafting bullet points (e.g., exaggerating numbers). **Always use a separate follow-up prompt** to ask the LLM to fact-check its work against the original source quotes. Do not combine the drafting and fact-checking into a single prompt.
*   **Human Review:** LLM self-checks are great but imperfect. You are the final editor. Review the final bullet points to ensure accuracy.

## 1.4.2 Refining and Synthesizing Content
If you already have a resume section but want to improve it using new source material:
1.  Extract "proposed bullet points" from the new source documents.
2.  **Organize First:** Instruct the LLM to group the new proposed bullet points under your *existing* resume bullet points based on topic relevance.
3.  **Merge:** Ask the LLM to blend the grouped ideas together into enhanced statements.



# 1.5 LLM-Assisted Memory Recall

Sometimes you need to update a resume section (like a past school project) but have no source documents available. In these cases, you are the source.

### 1.5.1 The Self-Interview Technique
1.  **Prompt for an Interview:** Ask the LLM to generate interview questions to help you recall details from memory. Provide brief context (e.g., "This was a group project for school") to ground the questions.
2.  **Provide Answers:** Answer the questions. (Using speech-to-text dictation can speed up this process immensely).
3.  **Draft Bullet Points:** Feed your answers back to the LLM and ask it to draft specific sections (e.g., project description, your individual contribution, project results, and a list of skills).
4.  **Self-Check and Save:** Run a self-check prompt to ensure no hallucinations were added. Save the detailed results in your Resume Notes, then ask the LLM to condense them into final resume bullet points.



# 1.6 Reviewing the Resume as a Whole

## 1.6.1 Extracting and Grouping Skills
To build a comprehensive "Skills" section, you need to harvest all the individual skills extracted during your earlier section-by-section analysis.
1.  **Aggregate:** Combine all extracted skills from work experience, projects, education, and your Resume Notes.
2.  **Extract First, Group Later:** Ask the LLM to do a pure extraction of all skills and label them with categories. Generating a table first gives the LLM a better chance of catching everything.
3.  **Generate Bullet Points:** Next, ask the LLM to group the categorized skills into bullet points under relevant group names (e.g., "Data Analysis:", "Project Management:"). 
4.  **1.6.2 Verification:** Prompt the LLM to run a specific self-check: "Check if any extracted skills are not found in the grouped bullet points." Be aware that this self-check can occasionally fail, so a quick human review is still necessary.

## 1.6.3 Revising the Summary (The Elevator Pitch)
Your resume summary is the 30-second elevator pitch that introduces you to recruiters.

1.  **Provide Full Context:** Give the LLM your entire finished resume (and your Resume Notes, if needed). 
2.  **Request a Critique & Draft:** Ask the LLM to critique your current summary, provide recommendations based on the whole resume, and propose a new draft.
3.  **Iterate and Direct:** The LLM might misunderstand your goals (e.g., identifying you as a "Cognitive Scientist" instead of a "Project Manager"). Have a follow-up conversation to correct its assumptions and provide explicit feedback about how you want to be presented.
4.  **Final Polish:** Do the final line edits yourself manually in your word processor. Do not rely entirely on the LLM to perfect the wording of your professional identity.
