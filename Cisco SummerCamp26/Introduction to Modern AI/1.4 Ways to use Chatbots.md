# 1.4 Ways to use Chatbots

## 1.4.1 Expand: Answer Questions

**Structured prompts:**

- Structure your question to get richer answers: compare and contrast, list, describe, explain
- Example: "Compare the main features of two phones" → the chatbot structures its own answer around comparison

**Sequence of subtasks:**

- Break the question into steps: list resources → collect key info → similarities → differences → conclusions
- Example: "Compare 2 smartphone models in under 2 minutes" works best when you first list reference sites, then ask for the comparison
- Follow up on answers, and split a large question into separate prompts when needed

**Reflection:**

- Ask the chatbot to double-check or revise its own work
- Note: the chatbot can't undo words it has already output - it can only revise what it says next

**Key-value format:**

- Example: asking about "breaking" vs "breakdancing" with the key/value format "words or terminology that describe this... / ..." prompts the chatbot to fill the blank
- Adding "(plural)" hints that you want multiple options

**Clarifying concepts:**

- "Proud of" vs. "humbled by": the chatbot distinguishes them (pride = achievement, humbled = modest gratitude)
- Claude even added an insight: saying "humbled by" can sound like false modesty

**General tip:**

- Try the same prompt on multiple chatbots and compare answers

## 1.4.2 Expand: Generating Ideas

**Ask for several options:**

- "Suggest several options for a joke about puppies" → multiple distinct jokes
- Analyze the structure of a joke, then generate similar ones:
  - "Two left feet" = idiom for being clumsy at dancing
  - Scarecrow joke: "outstanding in his field" (a pun on standing out in a field)
- Try explaining jokes to the chatbot and judge which ones it finds funny

**Fortune teller persona:**

- Persona: a fortune teller telling an inspiring story about your future life
- Give it your name, interests, hobbies, values, and pronoun
- The chatbot weaves these into an inspiring narrative
- Here, hallucination is a *feature*: it's daydreaming a plausible, motivating future

## 1.4.3 Synthesize: Summarize

**NotebookLM (Google):**

- Upload documents or paste links, then chat with Gemini about them
- Citations verify where each statement came from - check them!
- Example: summarizing Banksy's activities related to social, geopolitical, and current events - then verifying the citations point to real sources

**Summarize vs. quote:**

- "Summarize" → the chatbot paraphrases in its own words
- "Extract relevant text as direct quotes" → exact wording from the source
- ChatGPT sometimes refuses direct quotes; some chatbots only summarize
- Many chatbots don't support web links directly - download the page as HTML and upload it instead

**Context window:**

- Context length differs per chatbot: ChatGPT (paperclip icon), Claude (5 files × 30MB), Command R+ (processed the HTML but was slow - lots of structure around the text)
- Traditional software would need keywords (climate, war, refugee); chatbots understand concepts even without the exact words
- Plain text is best for the context window: Gemini ~26 pages, Mistral ~51 pages, Meta AI ~14 pages, Copilot ~4 pages (max chars vary)
- Claude accepted ~52 pages of pasted text (plain text works better than HTML)

**Delimiters for pasted text:**

- Backticks (```), three hashtags (###), or three double/single quotes (""" or ''')
- Tell the chatbot: "Treat the text between the delimiters as the content to process"
- Privacy note: paste only public content into hosted chatbots; for private content, use downloadable models

## 1.4.4 Synthesize: Label

- Label pieces of text by topic or keyword (e.g., "love", "positive", "excited", "negative", "sad")
- Group text by its labels
- Summarize the key points per group

## 1.4.5 Transform: Translate

**Multi-language translation:**

- Ask for a translation into several languages at once (Copilot: 9 languages in one answer)

**Follow-ups:**

- Ask "Is this a common idiom?" - the chatbot double-checks and may suggest native idioms
- Example: "blessing in disguise" → the Chinese idiom 塞翁失马 (the old man lost his horse) - a story about a horse, a war, and a broken leg, illustrating that fortune can turn into misfortune and vice versa

**Idiomatic translation:**

- Ask directly for the idiomatic translation plus options, labeled figurative vs. literal
- French example (Mistral): "a piece of cake" → literal slice + figuratives ("a game for children", "as easy as saying hello", "finger up the nose")
- Ask which figurative is most commonly used

**Morse code example:**

- Prompt: convert text to Morse code
- First attempt was wrong (S = dot-dash-dot? No - S is dot-dot-dot; the model mixed it up)
- Fix: ask for the Morse alphabet first, then do the conversion yourself with it
- Verify against Wikipedia

## 1.4.6 Transform: Reword and Revise

**Rewording emails:**

- Ask for American English, accessible vocabulary, positive tone - while maintaining intent
- The chatbot rewrites the draft, preserving the meaning

**Cleaning up speech recognition transcripts:**

- Speech recognition produces homophone errors (e.g., "alright" vs. "all right", "porch" vs. "pours" - wrong words that sound right)
- Ask the chatbot to fix these and remove filler words
- Set expectations: preserve the original style, tone, and word choices

**Improving your own prompts:**

- Ask the chatbot to rewrite your prompt to be clearer (e.g., translate to specific languages, quote exact phrasing, keep the same meaning)

## 1.4.7 Evaluate: Fact check

**The birthday cake + five second rule example:**

- Ask: "rewrite the email without grammar mistakes" → the chatbot only fixed grammar, leaving the factually wrong content (five second rule myth)
- Ask: "Is this factually correct?" → the chatbot explains the myth: bacteria transfer doesn't pause in five seconds
- Lesson: the *task* must be stated clearly - "Is this factually correct?" makes the goal explicit

**Reflection for fact checking:**

- Ask the chatbot to check its own response (logic problem: the chatbot initially says "Yes" to the 10 people/9 seats question, then when asked to re-check, corrects to "No")

## 1.4.8 Evaluate: Grading

**Grading with a rubric:**

- Teacher role: work out the solution yourself, compare to the student's answer, and give feedback with hints
- Logic problem: student = Meta AI's Llama; grader = the same model (Meta grading Meta) - the grader got it wrong (said "Yes")
- Qwen (Alibaba) as grader: answered correctly ("No"), explained why the student's reasoning failed, and gave hints
- **Graders can be wrong** - iterate and compare graders

## 1.4.9 Evaluate: Qualitative scoring

**The debate:**

- "Pineapples are better than anchovies as pizza toppings"
- Participants (open models on HuggingChat): Llama, Qwen, Command R+, Phi, Mistral NeMo
- Judges (proprietary models):
  - **Gemini**: criteria - argument strength, evidence, relevance, creativity, appeal; scores 8-9 per participant
  - **Claude**: criteria - structure, persuasiveness, evidence, counterarguments, creativity, delivery, relevance; totals 54-59
  - **ChatGPT**: criteria - clarity, persuasiveness, relevance, creativity, evidence, engagement; totals 48-55
- Different judges gave different scores and rankings - like real life, evaluation is subjective

**Model graded evaluation:**

- Send one chatbot's output to another chatbot for evaluation
- The judge model scores and ranks the participants

## 1.4.10 Chat: How's it going?

**Life coach role:**

- Persona: life coach - nonjudgmental, ask the human for their ideas first, don't wrap up the conversation, no internal monologue
- Default chatbots jump straight to solutions; the coach persona focuses on feelings
- Conversation flow example: breath mints → devastated → gently suggests alternatives → dental care advice → emotional support
- Try it without the role first to see the default behavior, then add the persona

## 1.4.11 Chat: Mock Interview

**Setting up the interview:**

- Give the chatbot the job description + interviewer role (e.g., cat cafe manager)
- Explicitly instruct: 3 questions, one at a time; ask a prepared question → wait for the answer → ask a follow-up → next question → conclude → give feedback
- **Why be so detailed?** Default behavior: the chatbot asks all questions at once and answers them itself
- Follow-ups simulate real interview dynamics; steer the conversation toward your strengths

**Feedback:**

- Ask for a rating (1-5) with improvement suggestions per question
- Practice receiving criticism graciously
- Gemma (open source, sibling of Gemini) via LM Studio keeps the interview private
- Bad answers get caught: "I'm allergic to cats" is not the best answer for a cat cafe job
- It doesn't always work perfectly - rewriting prompts is good practice

## 1.4.12 Chat: Chatbot to Chatbot Collaboration

**The writer:**

- Command R+ with a persona: writer for the Daily Bugle, humorous tone
- Task: opinion piece on superheroes cleaning up the city
- Signed: "your friendly neighborhood writer"

**The editor:**

- Mistral Large 2, editor role
- Feedback: what's good (tone, examples), what to improve (facts/numbers, sources), extra examples (Iron Man suit, Captain America, Thor weather), wording suggestions (reality check, head spin, not asking for the moon), eyewitness/expert interviews
- Explicit instruction: do NOT rewrite - just comment
- Balanced articles persuade better (psychology)

**The revision loop:**

- Writer accepts/rejects feedback with reasons (rejected a counterargument for tone)
- Rewrite incorporated suggestions + "extraordinary powers → extraordinary responsibilities" (Spider-Man reference)
- Second review: editor checks what was incorporated and what wasn't; says more revision needed

**Human in the loop:**

- The writer planned fictional quotes and interviews - a real news organization must publish facts, not fiction
- The editor missed this! Human oversight caught it
- Automation exists, but humans must oversee ambiguous and open-ended tasks

## 1.4.13 Take Actions: Tools

**The brain and arms analogy:**

- LLM = the brain; **tools** = the arms and legs (internet search, apps)
- The LLM can't browse the internet by itself - it uses tools

**How web search tools work:**

1. Model outputs formatted text: {TOOL: web_search, QUERY: ...}
2. Other software (the "arms") sees this and redirects to the search tool
3. The tool runs, results come back
4. Results + the original question are sent back to the model
5. The model answers using the search results

**With vs. without tools:**

- Phi without search: "Paris Olympics" → answered about the 1900 Olympics
- Phi with search: correct 2024 athletes + cited sources

**LLMs vs. traditional code:**

- LLMs are **probabilistic**: same input can produce different output; they adapt to typos and never print error messages
- Traditional code is **deterministic**: same input = same output; strict syntax, error messages on mistakes

**Code generation (Hello World):**

- Python needs parentheses and a comma: print("Hello, World!")
- The LLM fixes and generates code without error messages; Colab has "Generate with AI" (powered by Gemini)

**Fibonacci example:**

- Sequence: 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8... each number is the sum of the previous two
- The LLM wrote recursive code: correct for the 20th (6765) but slow for large numbers
- Ask "is this efficient?" → it explains the inefficiency
- Why use code: consistent, computationally efficient, less electricity and carbon; use a calculator for simple math
- Why the LLM wrote inefficient code: CS courses teach the recursive version first, so it dominates the training data

**Practical workflow:**

- Reddit parsing table: first run returned empty comments, second run worked → save the working code

## 1.4.14 Image Generation

**Diffusion models:**

- Start from pure noise, iteratively remove noise, guided by the text prompt, until the image emerges
- Like sculpting: "I saw the angel in the marble and carved until I set him free" (Michelangelo)

**Prompt specificity matters:**

- "Pineapple cake" (Meta AI) → a generic cake; "Taiwanese pineapple cake" → rectangular, flaky pastry with pineapple filling
- "A frog holding a red balloon" + "in the style of Banksy" → cartoonish street-art style
- ChatGPT calls its image generator DALL-E 3; ask for a stress ball variant and it varies the object

**Copyright and responsibility:**

- "In the style of a famous artist" can trigger content policy restrictions
- Be careful with realistic photos of people - society relies on photographs reflecting reality

## 1.4.15 Take Actions: Personal Assistants

**Siri, Google Assistant, Alexa:**

- Combine speech recognition + language models + tools
- **Calendar**: ask it to check your events, then add an event (confirms title and time)
- **Text messages**: compose, edit before sending (check the question mark - the assistant can change the meaning)
- **Specific apps**: "use Google Maps" → locate, directions, navigate. Without the app name, results differ
- Try: play music, set a reminder

## 1.4.16 Multimodal: Prompting with Images and Text

**LMMs (Large Multimodal Models):**

- Historically: an image captioning model + an LLM combined
- Now: one model that takes both images and text as input
- All task categories work with images: expand, synthesize, transform, evaluate, chat, actions

**Context matters in images:**

- Food photo on a white paper towel → "Taiwanese pineapple cake"
- Same dish on a bluish-gray plate → "Indian sweets" (so papdi, Kaju Katli)
- The background changed the interpretation!

**Painting analysis:**

- Describe the painting, interpret it (a child's view of the world), name it ("Twinkle Twinkle Little Frog")
- The model recognizes Starry Night style + a green frog

**Parking signs:**

- The chatbot asked for the day, time, and permit before answering
- Then: "Yes, you can park" with explanation (no fact checking needed - it was right)

**Menu translation:**

- Translate each item, show the original, and describe each dish

**Photo evaluation:**

- Ask for criteria-based scores with reasons
- The model identified dahlias in the photo

**Travel planning:**

- Photo of a park sign (Montreal): ask where to visit first
- The chatbot asks about interests, then web-searches photography spots
