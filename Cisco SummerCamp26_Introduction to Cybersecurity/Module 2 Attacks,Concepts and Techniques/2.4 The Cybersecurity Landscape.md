# 2.4.1 Cryptocurrency

**Cryptocurrency** is a form of **digital currency** that allows people to send and receive money securely over the internet. Instead of being controlled by banks or governments, cryptocurrencies use **cryptography** and a **blockchain** to verify and record transactions.

## How It Works

When someone sends cryptocurrency:

1. The transaction is requested.
2. It is verified by computers called **miners**.
3. The verified transaction is added to the **blockchain**.
4. The cryptocurrency is transferred to the recipient's digital wallet.

Because every transaction is recorded on the blockchain, it is extremely difficult to alter or fake.

## Key Terms

### Digital Wallet

A **digital wallet** securely stores your cryptocurrency and allows you to send or receive digital money.

Think of it as an online version of your physical wallet.

### Blockchain

A **blockchain** is a decentralized digital ledger that records every cryptocurrency transaction.

Unlike a bank database, copies of the blockchain are stored on thousands of computers worldwide, making it highly secure and difficult to tamper with.

### Mining

**Mining** is the process of verifying cryptocurrency transactions by solving complex mathematical problems.

Miners use powerful computers to:

- Verify transactions.
- Add new blocks to the blockchain.
- Help secure the network.

As a reward, miners usually receive cryptocurrency.

**Remember:** *Mining secures the blockchain and confirms transactions.*

## Key Features of Cryptocurrency

- Digital money with no physical form.
- Uses encryption to secure transactions.
- Stored in digital wallets.
- Transactions are recorded on a blockchain.
- Verified through mining.
- Not controlled by a central authority.

# 2.4.2 Cryptojacking

**Cryptojacking** is a cyberattack where attackers secretly use someone else's device to mine cryptocurrency without their knowledge or permission.

Instead of using their own computers, attackers steal your device's processing power to make money.

## How It Happens

A device can become infected by:

- Visiting a malicious website.
- Downloading infected software.
- Opening a malicious email attachment.
- Running malware in the background.

Once infected, the malware quietly mines cryptocurrency while your device is running.

## Common Signs of Cryptojacking

- Computer becomes unusually slow.
- High CPU or GPU usage.
- Device overheats.
- Battery drains quickly.
- Fans run constantly.
- Increased electricity usage.


## Prevention Tips

- Keep your operating system and software updated.
- Use reputable antivirus software.
- Avoid suspicious websites and downloads.
- Be cautious when opening email attachments.
- Monitor CPU usage for unusual activity.

**Remember:** *Cryptojacking doesn't steal your money directly—it steals your computer's processing power to earn cryptocurrency for the attacker.*
