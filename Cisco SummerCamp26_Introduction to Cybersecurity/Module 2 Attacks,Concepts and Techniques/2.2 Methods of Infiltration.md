# 2.2.1 Social Engineering

**Social engineering** is the art of manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that benefit an attacker. Instead of hacking computers, attackers **hack human psychology**, taking advantage of trust, fear, curiosity, or urgency.

## Common Types of Social Engineering

### Pretexting

Pretexting is when an attacker **creates a fake story or identity** to convince someone to reveal sensitive information.

**Example:**
Someone pretends to be from your bank or IT department and asks you to verify your account details.

**Remember:** *Fake story = Real information.*

### Tailgating

Tailgating is when an unauthorized person **follows an authorized person into a restricted area** without proper permission.

**Example:**
Someone asks you to hold the door because they "forgot their access card."

**Remember:** *Don't hold secure doors open for strangers.*

 

### Quid Pro Quo (Something for Something)

In a quid pro quo attack, the attacker offers **something valuable in exchange for sensitive information.**

Examples include:

- Free gifts
- Free software
- Technical support
- Rewards

**Remember:** *If something seems free, your information may be the real payment.*
# 2.2.2 Denial-of-Service (DoS)

A **Denial-of-Service (DoS)** attack attempts to make a computer, server, or network **unavailable** by overwhelming it with excessive traffic or requests.

### Goal

- Slow down services
- Crash systems
- Prevent legitimate users from accessing resources

**Remember:** *One attacker overwhelms one target.*

# 2.2.3 Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS)

A **Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS)** attack works like a DoS attack but uses **many infected devices** instead of just one.

Attackers control thousands of compromised computers (called **bots or zombies**) that all attack the target at the same time.

### How it works

1. Devices become infected with malware.
2. They join a **botnet**.
3. The attacker commands all infected devices to attack one target simultaneously.

**Remember:** *DoS = One attacker. DDoS = Thousands of attackers.*
# 2.2.4 Botnet

A **botnet** is a network of malware-infected devices that are remotely controlled by an attacker.

Devices usually become bots after:

- Opening a malicious email attachment.
- Downloading infected software.
- Visiting a malicious website.

Botnets are commonly used to:

- Launch DDoS attacks.
- Spread malware.
- Send spam emails.
- Perform brute-force attacks.

**Remember:** *A botnet is an army of infected computers.*

# 2.2.5 On-Path Attack (Man-in-the-Middle)

An **On-Path Attack**, also called a **Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)** attack, occurs when an attacker secretly intercepts communication between two parties.

The attacker can:

- Read data.
- Modify messages.
- Steal credentials.
- Pretend to be either side.

**Example:**
Connecting to an insecure public Wi-Fi where an attacker intercepts your traffic.

**Remember:** *The attacker sits "in the middle" of the conversation.*
# 2.2.6 SEO Poisoning

**SEO Poisoning** is when attackers manipulate search engine results so that malicious websites appear near the top.

When users click the fake result, they may:

- Download malware.
- Visit phishing websites.
- Reveal personal information.

**Remember:** *Top search result doesn't always mean safe.*

# 2.2.7 Wi-Fi Password Cracking

Attackers may try to obtain a Wi-Fi password through **social engineering** rather than technical hacking.

### Scenario Question

A colleague asks for the **private Wi-Fi password** because they cannot connect.

**Correct Answer:**

> **"I'm not sure we're allowed to use the private Wi-Fi network. Let me check with my manager first."**

### Why?

Never share passwords without proper authorization, even with coworkers.
# 2.2.8 Password Attacks

Passwords are one of the most common targets for attackers. Several techniques are used to crack or steal them.

## Password Spraying

Attempts **one common password** (like `Password123`) across many user accounts.

**Goal:** Avoid account lockouts.

## Dictionary Attack

Tries passwords from a list of common words and phrases.

Examples:

- football
- password
- qwerty
- welcome123

## Brute-Force Attack

Tries **every possible password combination** until the correct one is found.

Stronger and longer passwords take much longer to crack.

## Rainbow Table Attack

Uses **precomputed password hashes** to quickly crack passwords.

Adding **salt** to passwords makes this attack much less effective.

## Traffic Interception

Attackers capture network traffic to steal usernames and passwords while they are transmitted.

Using **HTTPS**, **VPNs**, and encryption helps prevent this attack.

# 2.2.9 Password Strength

The stronger a password is, the longer it takes to crack.

Strong passwords should include:

- Uppercase letters
- Lowercase letters
- Numbers
- Special characters
- Longer length (12–16+ characters)

### Password Strength Order (Weakest → Strongest)

1. `Password`
2. `3trawberry`
3. `K4km9n2R`
4. `H$1gh#7iD@3`

**Remember:** *Length and complexity greatly increase cracking time.*

# 2.2.10 Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)

An **Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)** is a **long-term, targeted cyberattack** carried out by skilled and well-funded attackers.

Their goal is to remain **hidden** inside a system while stealing valuable information over time.

### Characteristics

- Highly sophisticated
- Stealthy
- Long-term
- Targets governments or large organizations
- Often state-sponsored or organized cybercriminals

**Remember:** *APTs don't smash the door—they quietly live inside the house.*

# 2.2.11 Scenario Practice

## Scenario 1

**Question:**

A stranger asks you to hold the secure office door because they forgot their access card.

**Answer:** **Social Engineering (Tailgating)**

## Scenario 2

**Question:**

Your computer displays:

> *"Your connection was interrupted. A network change was detected."*

**Answer:** **Denial-of-Service (DoS)**

## Scenario 3

**Question:**

You search for your company's website on Google, but the first result redirects you to a fake antivirus page.

**Answer:** **SEO Poisoning**
